Atoms

These notes are a short overview of atomic structure. A full lesson on nuclear physics is still in development.

atomic structure

An atom has a small nucleus containing protons and neutrons, surrounded by electrons. Protons have positive charge, electrons have negative charge, and neutrons have no electric charge.

The table gives the charges and masses of the three main parts of an atom.

The number of protons determines the element. The periodic table organizes elements by that number.

An atom is electrically neutral when it has equal numbers of protons and electrons. Gaining or losing electrons makes an ion. Atoms of the same element with different numbers of neutrons are called isotopes.

Chemical bonds involve the electrons around atoms. Atoms can share electrons or transfer them, producing bonds that hold substances together.

Electrons in atoms have allowed energy states. Transitions between these states can absorb or emit photons, as discussed in the quantum notes.

Nuclear Fission

In fission, a heavy nucleus splits into smaller nuclei and can release neutrons and energy. Both charge and the total number of protons and neutrons must balance in a nuclear reaction.

Chain Reactions

A fission chain reaction occurs when neutrons from one fission event cause further nuclei to split. A sustained reaction needs enough of those neutrons to cause another fission.

Radiation

Unstable nuclei can emit particles or photons as they decay. Alpha radiation consists of helium nuclei, beta radiation includes electrons or positrons, and gamma radiation consists of high energy photons.

More Practice

Example: A carbon atom has 6 protons and 6 electrons. What is its net electric charge?
solution $$q = (6-6)(1.6 \times 10^{-19})$$ $$q = 0\,\mathrm{C}$$

The positive and negative charges cancel, so the atom is neutral.

Example: A carbon atom has 6 protons and 6 electrons. Estimate its total mass by adding the masses of its particles.
solution

We also need the number of neutrons. Carbon isotopes have the same number of protons but different numbers of neutrons, so the given information does not determine the mass.

Question: An atom loses one electron. Has it become an isotope of a different element, or an ion of the same element?
answer

It is a positive ion of the same element. The number of protons has not changed, and neither has the number of neutrons. Only its electron count and net charge have changed.
